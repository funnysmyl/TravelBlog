Copenhagen in 3 Days: Big Experiences, Smart Spending

Discover Copenhagen, where historic charm meets modern design, vibrant cycling culture and waterfront living. Experience the heart of Scandinavia through cozy cafés, innovative cuisine, and effortlessly cool city life.

I've been traveling since 2017, chasing moments instead of plans. I'm fascinated by snakes, love sharks, would happily spend weeks deep in the jungle, and can never get enough papaya. As long as there's a next destination, I know I'm exactly where I'm meant to be.

Copenhagen has a reputation for being one of Europe's most expensive cities – and yes, a coffee here can cost more than you're used to. But with a little planning, three days in the Danish capital can be packed with unforgettable moments without emptying your wallet. Here's how I spent mine.

Day one belongs to the classics: a stroll along the colourful houses of Nyhavn, a walk down the pedestrian street Strøget and a picnic in the King's Garden next to Rosenborg Castle. On day two I swapped the map for a bike and followed the harbour – past the Opera House, through the alternative neighbourhood of Christiania and on to the street food halls at Reffen. The city is flat, the bike lanes are wide, and you'll see more in one afternoon than in a whole day on foot.

Three ways to experience more and spend less:

  • Rent a bike instead of taking taxis. Copenhagen is built for cycling, and most sights are only a few minutes apart.
  • Get the Copenhagen Card if you plan to visit several museums. It includes public transport and free entry to many attractions.
  • Go for a swim in the harbour baths – places like Islands Brygge are free to use in summer.

On my last day I took it slow: cinnamon buns for breakfast, smørrebrød for lunch and a canal tour to see the city from the water. In the evening I ended up at Tivoli Gardens, where the lights come on just as the sun goes down. Copenhagen isn't a city you rush through – it's one you settle into, and three days are just enough to understand why the Danes are so good at feeling at home.

Pro tipps

  1. Skip bottled water: Copenhagen's tap water is excellent, so bring a refillable bottle and save money every day.
  2. Eat out at lunchtime: Many restaurants offer lunch menus that cost far less than dinner. You can find more ideas on Visit Copenhagen.
